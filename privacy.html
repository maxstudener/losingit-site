Privacy Policy

Effective November 13, 2026 · Updated November 18, 2026

This policy explains how the Losing It app for iPhone (“the app”) handles your information. Losing It is made and sold by Powerup Gaming LLC (“we”, “us”).

The short version

  • There is no account or sign-in.
  • We do not collect, see, sell or share any of your personal data.
  • There are no analytics, no ads and no tracking of any kind.
  • Your journal and food log live on your iPhone and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud database, which only you can access.
  • When you search for a food, the words you type are never sent anywhere. The app downloads small numbered files from our public food index, hosted by Cloudflare, and we do not log or store searches. Everyday foods can be found offline.
  • When you scan or type a food barcode, or import a receipt with barcodes printed on it, the app checks the same index by a bucket number first. If needed, only that barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts to look the product up. Nothing about you goes with it.
  • Recipes and receipts you import are read on your iPhone. The photos, scans, PDFs and text you import are never uploaded. To read a recipe from a link, your iPhone opens that page straight from its website, with no cookies and nothing from your journal.

What the app keeps, and where

To work, the app stores what you enter:

  • Your journey: the name you enter, your starting weight, goal weight and start date, and any daily targets you choose to set (calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber and water).
  • Weigh-ins: each weight with its date, and any mood or note you add.
  • Progress photos: the photos you add, with their date, any caption you write, and the weight from the closest weigh-in.
  • Food log: each food you log, with its name, brand, calories and nutrients (such as protein, carbs, fat, fiber, sugars, saturated fat and sodium) when they are known, servings, meal and date, its barcode if it has one, and which food database entry or saved food it came from.
  • Water log: each glass or amount of water you add, with its date.
  • Saved foods: foods you star as favorites, foods you create yourself, recipes you build or import, and meals you save, with their nutrition and how often you use them. An imported recipe also keeps the name of the website it came from, or its domain, but not the link to the recipe.
  • Recipes waiting to be checked: recipes you share to Losing It from another app, kept until you save them or set them aside, or Losing It clears them, as described under Recipe import.
  • Settings: your reminder time, meal reminder times, water glass size, favorite affirmations and the Glow Coach switch.

On your iPhone

All of this is stored in the app’s own storage on your device. Recipes waiting to be checked are kept in a private folder on your device that only Losing It can open.

In your iCloud, if iCloud is on

If you are signed in to iCloud, the app syncs your journey, weigh-ins, photos, food log, water log, and saved foods, recipes and meals through Apple’s CloudKit to the private database of your own iCloud account, so they can appear on your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account. That private database belongs to you: only you can access it, and we cannot see it. Apple’s handling of iCloud data is covered by Apple’s Privacy Policy. Your settings (reminder times, water glass size, favorite affirmations and the Glow Coach switch) are not synced and stay on each device. Recipes waiting to be checked are not synced either; they stay on the iPhone you shared them from.

Nothing comes to us

We do not run any servers that receive your information. Our food index is a set of ready-made files hosted by Cloudflare; it only hands out files and has no code that could receive, log or store your searches. The app contains no analytics, advertising, crash-reporting or other third-party code. Apart from your own iCloud, the food index and barcode lookups described under Food search and Barcode lookups, and the recipe pages it opens for you, described under Recipe import, the app does not connect to anything, and it never sends your information to us or to anyone else. If you have chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers, Apple may give us aggregated usage statistics and crash reports for the app. That sharing is run by Apple under your iOS settings, not by code in the app, and it does not include what you write in your journal.

Camera and photos

The camera is used only for progress photos, for scanning food barcodes, and for scanning cookbook pages and receipts. Your photo library is used only for progress photos, recap cards, and the recipe or receipt pictures you pick to import. Both are used only when you choose to:

  • Camera: iOS asks for your permission the first time you tap the camera button to take a progress photo, or the first time you scan a barcode, a cookbook page or a receipt. While you scan a barcode, the camera view is read on your iPhone to find the barcode. No picture is saved or sent. Only the barcode number is used, as described under Barcode lookups. Cookbook pages and receipts you scan are read on your iPhone, then let go. They are not saved or sent. If a receipt has barcodes printed on it, some of those numbers may be sent to Open Food Facts, as described under Receipt import.
  • Photo library: the app uses Apple’s photo picker, so it receives only the photos you pick. It does not get access to the rest of your library. Pictures you pick to import a recipe or receipt are read on your iPhone and are not saved or sent. Here too, barcode numbers printed on a receipt may be sent to Open Food Facts, as described under Receipt import.
  • Saving to Photos: if you use the share button to save a progress photo or recap card to your photo library, iOS asks for permission to add it. That permission only lets the app add images; it cannot read your library.

The app saves a resized copy of each progress photo in your journal and reads the date the photo was taken so it lands in the right place on your timeline. The copy it keeps does not include the photo’s location or other camera details. Photos stay on your device and, if iCloud is on, in your private iCloud database.

Food search

When you search for a food on the Food tab, the app looks in two places:

  • Built into the app: everyday foods, such as fruit, vegetables, eggs and bread, are included in the app itself, so you can find them on your iPhone even without a connection.
  • Our public food index: for more foods and packaged products, the app downloads small pieces of a public food index that Powerup Gaming publishes at losingit-food.pages.dev. The index is hosted by Cloudflare and is fetched over an encrypted HTTPS connection.

The index is split into thousands of numbered files. For each word you search for, your iPhone works out a bucket number from that word and downloads the file with that number, plus a small file that says which edition of the index is current. Many different words share each bucket number. Your iPhone then finds the matching foods in those files and ranks them itself.

  • What is sent: only requests for numbered files. The words you type are never sent, to us or to anyone else.
  • What is not sent: no search text, name, weight, photos, food log, account or device identifier.
  • What we do with searches: nothing. We do not log, store or analyse searches, and we never receive what you search for.
  • What Cloudflare can see: Cloudflare is the hosting provider that delivers the files. Like any web host, it processes each request, including the internet (IP) address it comes from and the numbered file it asks for, in order to deliver that file. How Cloudflare handles that is covered by the Cloudflare privacy policy.

A food you pick from the results is added to your food log like any other food: on your iPhone and, if iCloud is on, in your private iCloud database.

Barcode lookups

When you scan or type a barcode on the Food tab, or a receipt you import has a barcode printed on it, the app looks the product up in up to two steps:

  • First, our food index: your iPhone works out a bucket number from the barcode and downloads the file with that number from the same Cloudflare-hosted index described under Food search. The barcode itself is not sent there.
  • Then, only if needed, Open Food Facts: if the index doesn’t have the product or can’t be reached, the app sends only that barcode number, over an encrypted HTTPS connection, to Open Food Facts, an open, non-profit food database. It asks for that product’s name, brand, serving size and nutrition facts (the app uses the calories and nutrients), and nothing else. For a receipt, this happens only when the item’s name finds nothing either, and for no more than 8 items per receipt.
  • What is not sent: no name, weight, photos, food log, account or device identifier. The request to Open Food Facts names the app, not you.
  • What Open Food Facts can see: like any website you visit, its servers can see the internet (IP) address the request comes from. How Open Food Facts handles that is covered by the Open Food Facts privacy policy.
  • When nothing is sent: if you scan or type a barcode you have logged before, the app uses your own entry and does not look it up again, unless that entry has calories but no other nutrients, such as a food you added yourself after a scan found nothing. Then it looks that barcode up again in the same two steps, in case its nutrients are known now. Foods you add again from Recent are never sent anywhere, and the name and calories you type for a food yourself are never sent.

The foods you log are stored like your weigh-ins: on your iPhone and, if iCloud is on, in your private iCloud database.

Recipe import

You can bring a recipe into Losing It by sharing it from Safari or another app, or with Import a recipe on the Food tab, where you can paste a link or a recipe’s text, choose screenshots or photos, open a PDF or file, or scan a cookbook page. Losing It reads the recipe, matches its ingredients to foods and shows it to you to check. Nothing is added to your foods until you save it.

  • Read on your iPhone: text, photos, screenshots, scanned pages, PDFs and files are read on your iPhone, with Apple’s built-in text recognition for pictures. They are never uploaded, to us or to anyone else. The app reads your clipboard only when you tap its Paste button.
  • Pages shared from Safari: Safari hands Losing It a copy of the page you are looking at, with its address, title, text and the recipe details the page publishes, so usually nothing needs to be downloaded. If that copy has no recipe in it, the app opens the page’s link itself, as described next.
  • Links: when a recipe comes as a link (one you paste or share from another app, or a single link in text with no ingredient list of its own), your iPhone opens that page straight from its website over an encrypted HTTPS connection, only to read the recipe. First, the app removes common tracking tags from the link, such as utm_ and fbclid tags, and anything after a #. If the website sends the app on to another address, as short links do, the app follows it there, up to 5 times, with the same protections each time. If the page has no recipe but points to the recipe’s main page on another website, the app may open that one page too. It never loads the pages’ pictures, ads or scripts.
  • What a website can see: the request carries no cookies, no sign-in, nothing from your journal and no account or device identifier. Like any website you visit, the recipe’s website, and any website the link passes through on the way, can see your internet (IP) address, the link it was asked for, and the usual details an iPhone app sends, such as the app’s name and version, your iOS version and your preferred language. How each one handles them is covered by that website’s own privacy policy.
  • Matching ingredients: each ingredient is matched on your iPhone with the everyday foods built into the app and, when they have no good match or the ingredient names a brand, with our food index in the private way described under Food search. Only numbered files are requested. The ingredient words are never sent.
  • Sharing from another app: when you tap Save to Losing It in the share sheet, what you shared waits in a private folder on your iPhone that only Losing It can open, ready for you to check in the app, until it is removed as described below. That copy holds the link and text you shared, any photos, PDF, saved web page or text file exactly as the other app handed them over (a photo keeps any location or camera details saved in it), and for a page from Safari, the page’s address, title, site name, language, text, any text you selected on it and the structured data it publishes, such as its recipe details. If you cancel instead, nothing is kept. Shared recipes are not part of your journal, so they don’t sync through iCloud, and they are never sent to us.
  • How long shared recipes are kept: a shared recipe is removed when you save it, when you tap the × beside it on the Shared with Losing It card to set it aside, or when the app finds it has no recipe it can read. Opening one without saving it keeps it waiting, and so does one whose link couldn’t be opened just then, so you can try again. Losing It keeps at most the 10 newest, clears any older than 7 days the next time you open the app, and removes them all when you erase everything.
  • The “ready to check” notification: if you already allow notifications from Losing It, saving a share shows a notification with the recipe’s name. It is made on your iPhone, and the share sheet never asks for notification permission itself. Like any notification, it can show on your Lock Screen.
  • What’s saved: when you save a recipe, the app keeps its name, its servings, its ingredients with their amounts and nutrition, and the name of the website it came from, or its domain, which also shows on servings you log. It does not keep the link, the page or text you imported, or any picture or file. Saved recipes are stored like your other saved foods: on your iPhone and, if iCloud is on, in your private iCloud database.

Receipt import

With Import a receipt on the Food tab, you can turn a grocery, delivery or restaurant receipt into a meal. Scan a paper receipt, choose screenshots or photos, open a PDF, picture, text or saved email file, or paste a receipt’s text. Losing It reads it, matches each item to a food and shows you the meal to check. Nothing is saved or added to your day until you choose to.

  • Read on your iPhone: receipts are read on your iPhone, with Apple’s built-in text recognition for pictures and scans. The receipt is never uploaded. From a saved email, the app reads the email’s text, or a text file attached to it in its place. Pictures and PDFs attached to the email are not used.
  • Spelling out shorthand: on iOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence turned on, the app may ask Apple’s on-device language model, the same one Glow Coach uses, to spell out short item names such as “WHL MLK”. It is given the item names and the store’s name, and it runs on your iPhone. This doesn’t depend on the Glow Coach switch; turning off Apple Intelligence in iOS Settings stops it.
  • Matching foods: item names, and for a restaurant receipt the restaurant’s name, are matched on your iPhone and with our food index in the private way described under Food search. Only numbered files are requested. The words are never sent.
  • Barcodes on a receipt: a barcode printed beside an item is looked up in our food index by a bucket number first. If the index doesn’t have it or can’t be reached, and the item’s name finds nothing either, that barcode number may be sent to Open Food Facts, as described under Barcode lookups, for no more than 8 items per receipt.
  • What’s saved: the receipt itself, its prices and its totals are never saved. The meal is named after the store, or “Receipt” and the date, until you rename it. If you save the meal, the app keeps its name and the foods in it, like your other saved meals. If you add it to your day, its foods are added to your food log. These are kept on your iPhone and, if iCloud is on, in your private iCloud database.

Where the food data comes from

The food index and the everyday foods built into the app are made from public food databases, including:

  • USDA FoodData Central from the U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service, which is in the public domain.
  • Open Food Facts, available under the Open Database License (ODbL), with individual entries under the Database Contents License.

The full list of sources and their licenses is on the food data credits page. Our food index is itself published openly at losingit-food.pages.dev.

Glow Coach

Glow Coach is an optional feature that writes short, encouraging notes. It uses Apple’s on-device language model (part of Apple Intelligence) and runs entirely on your iPhone. The notes are written from a few facts about your journey, such as your first name, pounds lost, pounds to go and your weigh-in streak. Nothing is sent to us or to any third party. You can turn Glow Coach off at any time in the app’s Settings. When it is off or unavailable on your device, the app shows its built-in encouragement instead.

Widgets

Losing It’s Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets read a small summary that the app saves on your device for them, such as your first name, your progress, your next bubbles, your streak, today’s Glow Coach note, and today’s calories, protein, carbs, fat and water with any targets you set. This summary stays on your device. Keep in mind that a Lock Screen widget can be seen by anyone who can see your Lock Screen.

Reminders

If you turn on daily weigh-in reminders or meal reminders, iOS asks for your permission to show notifications. Reminders are scheduled on your iPhone itself; we do not send them and never learn when or whether you weigh in or eat.

Sharing

If you choose to share a recap card or a progress photo with the share button, iOS sends it to the app or person you pick. What happens next is up to you and that service. The app never shares anything on its own. Sharing a recipe into Losing It from another app is described under Recipe import.

Deleting your data

Because we never receive your data, there is nothing for us to delete on our side. You are in full control:

  • In the app: tap the gear icon on the Journey tab to open Settings, then tap Erase everything & start fresh. This deletes your journey, weigh-ins, photos, food log, water log, and saved foods, recipes and meals, and clears the widgets, reminders, favorite affirmations and any recipes waiting to be checked. If iCloud sync is on, the erase also removes your journal from iCloud and from every device signed in to that iCloud account. It cannot be undone.
  • Delete the app: deleting Losing It removes everything it stored on that iPhone. A copy synced to iCloud stays there until you erase it in the app or in iOS Settings.
  • Manage iCloud data: in the iOS Settings app, tap your name, then iCloud. From there you can manage your iCloud storage and delete the data saved by Losing It, or turn off iCloud for the app in the list of apps that use iCloud.

Children

Losing It is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children, because we do not collect personal information at all.

Health disclaimer

Losing It is a personal journal for tracking and encouragement. It is not a medical device and does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. Glow Coach notes and affirmations are encouragement, not health guidance. The optional daily calorie target is a number you choose; the app does not recommend one, and calorie numbers from package labels, food databases, imported recipes or receipts may not be exact. Talk with a doctor or other qualified health professional before starting or changing a weight-loss plan, especially if you are pregnant, have a health condition, or have a history of disordered eating.
