Frequently asked questions

How does iCloud sync work, and why isn’t my data syncing?

Losing It syncs your journey, weigh-ins, photos, food log, water log, and saved foods, recipes and meals automatically through your own private iCloud. For sync to work:

  • Be signed in to iCloud in the Settings app, with iCloud Drive turned on.
  • Use the same Apple Account on each device.
  • Make sure Losing It is allowed to use iCloud (Settings → your name → iCloud, in the list of apps using iCloud).
  • Be connected to the internet.

Changes can take a few minutes to show up on your other devices, and photos can take longer. Opening the app on both devices while on Wi-Fi helps. You can check the sync status in Losing It under Settings → iCloud.

Your reminder times, water glass size, favorite affirmations and the Glow Coach switch are saved per device and don’t sync. Recipes you’ve shared to Losing It but haven’t saved yet wait on the iPhone you shared them from.

I got a new iPhone. How do I get my journey back?
  1. Sign in to the new iPhone with the same Apple Account and turn on iCloud and iCloud Drive.
  2. Download Losing It from the App Store. Your purchase is tied to your Apple Account, so you won’t pay again.
  3. Open the app. It shows “Looking for your journey in iCloud” while your journey comes over. Photos may take a little longer to arrive.

If the welcome screen appears instead, your journey may not have arrived yet. Close the app, wait a few minutes on Wi-Fi, and open it again. If you already set up a new journey before the old one arrived, the app keeps your original journey once both are synced, along with any weigh-ins you logged in the meantime.

How do I add a widget?

Home Screen: touch and hold an empty spot on your Home Screen until the apps jiggle, tap Edit, then Add Widget. Search for Losing It and pick Pound Bubbles (small, medium or large), Daily Affirmation (small or medium), Today’s Food (small), Food & Macros (medium) or Water (small), then tap Add Widget.

Lock Screen: touch and hold your Lock Screen, tap Customize, choose the Lock Screen, tap the widget area, and pick Glow Progress or Today’s Food from Losing It. Anyone who can see your Lock Screen can see what these widgets show.

Widgets update when you log a weigh-in, a food or a glass of water. If one looks out of date, open the app for a moment. Until you’ve set up your journey in the app, widgets say “Open Losing It to begin.”

Why don’t I see Glow Coach?

Glow Coach writes its notes with Apple’s on-device model, so it needs iOS 26 or later on an iPhone that supports Apple Intelligence, with Apple Intelligence turned on (Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri). Right after you turn Apple Intelligence on, the model may still be downloading, so give it a little time.

You can check Glow Coach’s status in Losing It under Settings → Glow Coach, and switch it off there with Personal pep talks. When Glow Coach isn’t available, Losing It shows its built-in encouragement and affirmations instead. Everything else works the same.

How do I edit or delete a weigh-in?

Go to the Progress tab and tap a weigh-in under Recent weigh-ins. You can change the weight, date, mood or note and tap Save changes, or tap Delete this weigh-in.

For older entries, tap See all (shown once you have more than five weigh-ins). Tap any entry to edit it, or swipe left on it for Edit and Delete.

How do I change my goal?

On the Journey tab, tap the gear icon at the top right to open Settings. Under Your journey you can change your goal weight, as well as your starting weight, name and start date. Your pound bubbles update to match.

Your goal needs to be far enough below your starting weight to leave at least one pound bubble to pop. If it isn’t, Settings tells you and keeps your previous weights until it is.

How do I search for a food?

On the Food tab, tap Add food, then type a food or a brand into the search field. Tap a result to see its calories, choose a portion or an amount, and add it to your meal.

  • Everyday foods work offline. Basics like fruit, vegetables, eggs, bread and rice are built into the app, so you can find them even with no signal.
  • Packaged foods need a connection. Brands and packaged products come from our online food index, so they show up when you’re connected.
  • Fewer words work best. Every word you type has to match, so “oatmeal” finds more than “a big bowl of oatmeal”.
  • For a packaged food, try the brand plus the product, such as the brand name followed by “greek yogurt” or “granola bar”.

Can’t find it? Scan its barcode, or tap Add it yourself and type the name and calories from the label. Searching never sends the words you type; see the privacy policy for how it works.

Where does the food data come from?

Search results, barcode lookups and the foods matched in imported recipes and receipts come from public food databases: USDA FoodData Central from the U.S. Department of Agriculture, which includes nutrition labels shared by food makers, Open Food Facts, an open food database filled in by volunteers, and any other sources listed on the food data credits page.

These numbers are a helpful starting point, but they can differ from your package, since recipes change and portions vary. If something looks off, tap Adjust the details before you add it, or tap the food later on the Food tab to edit it. For an imported recipe or receipt, tap a food while you check it to change its amount or choose Find a different match.

Where do protein, carbs and the other nutrients come from?

Foods from search and barcode lookups come with the nutrients their database lists: protein, carbs, fat, fiber, sugars, saturated fat and sodium. Open a food to see them for the portion you pick. Carbs are total carbohydrate, including fiber, as on US labels. For foods you add yourself, macros are optional.

Some foods don’t list every nutrient. Those show a dash, and the day’s totals say how many of your foods had them. Like calories, nutrient numbers come from labels and public databases and may not be exact.

How do favorites, my own foods and recipes work?
  • Favorites: tap the star on any food. Starred foods show up under Favorites in Add food and open at the amount you logged last time.
  • Your own foods: in Add food, choose Create a food and type the name, serving and calories (macros are optional).
  • Recipes: build one from foods you search for, your own foods or anything you type in, or import one from a web page, a photo or a PDF. Say how many servings it makes, and Losing It works out each serving. Then log a serving whenever you have it.
  • Meals: save the foods you often have together, like your usual breakfast, and add them all in one tap.
  • Quick add: when you only know the calories, Quick add logs just the number.

To repeat a meal, open a meal’s menu on the Food tab and choose Same as yesterday, or copy a past meal to today. To keep it for any day, choose Save as a meal. Touch and hold a food to duplicate it or move it to another meal.

How do I import a recipe?

You can bring a recipe in from almost anywhere, and you’ll check every ingredient before anything is saved.

  • From Safari: open the recipe, tap the Share button, choose Losing It, then tap Save to Losing It.
  • From a recipe app, Photos or Files: share the recipe, up to 5 photos or screenshots, or its PDF to Losing It the same way. Photos may not keep the order you picked them in, so for several screenshots, Choose screenshots or photos in the app keeps your order. In a recipe app, you can also copy the recipe’s link and paste it into Losing It.
  • From Notes, Messages or a social post: copy the recipe’s text, including the ingredients, and paste it into Losing It.
  • In the app: on the Food tab, tap Add food, then Import a recipe. From there you can paste a link or a recipe’s text, choose up to 5 screenshots or photos in order, open a PDF or file, or scan up to 5 cookbook pages with your camera.

After you share a recipe, open Losing It. The newest recipe you shared in the last half hour opens by itself. Others wait under Shared with Losing It in Add food and on the Import a recipe screen, which show the 3 newest at a time. Losing It keeps the 10 newest, for up to 7 days. If you allow notifications from Losing It, you can also tap “Your recipe is ready to check”.

Losing It matches each ingredient and its amount to a food. Tap anything marked Needs a match or Check the amount to fix it, and set how many servings the recipe makes. Then tap Just save the recipe, or save it and add a serving to your meal right away. Your recipe goes into My foods, ready whenever you have it.

Losing It couldn’t find a recipe’s ingredients.
  • Web pages: open the page in Safari and share it to Losing It from there. Some sites only open in a browser, so a link pasted from somewhere else may not open. You can also copy the ingredients and paste them in, or take a screenshot of them.
  • Photos and screenshots: make sure the whole ingredient list is in view, flat and well lit. For a long recipe, choose a few screenshots in order.
  • Social posts: copy the caption with the ingredients and paste it in.
  • Very short recipes: a photo or PDF of a recipe with a single ingredient counted in pieces, like “Scrambled eggs, 3 eggs”, says it couldn’t find the ingredients. Copy and paste its text instead, and it reads fine.
  • No connection: opening a link needs the internet. Tap Try again once you’re back online, or paste the recipe’s text instead.

An ingredient Losing It can’t match is marked Needs a match. Tap it to search for a food that matches. To leave it out, touch and hold it and choose Remove. To leave out every ingredient without a match at once, use the remove button under the list. Without a match it counts as 0 calories.

How do I import a receipt?

A receipt comes in as a meal, so a grocery haul or a takeout order becomes foods you can log in one go. On the Food tab, tap Add food, then Import a receipt. You can:

  • Scan a paper receipt with your camera. Lay it flat in good light. A long one can go in a few pages.
  • Choose screenshots or photos, up to 5 in order. In a delivery app, open the order, then its receipt. In a grocery app, look in your purchase history.
  • Open a PDF or file, such as a receipt PDF or an email saved as a PDF.
  • Paste receipt text copied from an email or web page.

Losing It finds the foods, matches each one, and shows them as a meal named after the store, or “Receipt” and the date when the store can’t be read. Check each food and its amount, and tap one to change it or find it a match. Then tap Just save the meal to keep it in My meals, or add it to your day, with or without saving it.

The receipt itself, its prices and its totals are never saved. A food without a match counts as 0 calories until you find one or leave it out.

How do saved meals work?

A saved meal is a group of foods you often have together, like your usual breakfast, that you can add in one tap.

  • Save one you’ve logged: on the Food tab, open a meal’s menu and choose Save as a meal.
  • Build one: in Add food, tap Build a meal in the My meals card. Once you have a meal, you’ll find it under the + beside My meals. Give it a name, then tap Add a food (then Add another) for each food. To start from a receipt, choose Import a receipt instead.
  • Log one: tap the + beside a meal in My meals to add all of it at once. Or tap the meal to see what’s in it, tap any food to leave it out this time, then add the rest. Each food is added on its own, so you can change or remove one later, and Undo takes the whole meal back right after.
  • Change one: tap the meal, then Edit the meal.

The Add a food screen in the meal builder works like Add food. Search for a food, Scan a barcode, Type a barcode, Add it yourself, use Quick add, or pick one of yours. A recent food goes in with one tap, and the screen stays open for more. You can also tap one of My meals to add all its foods, Import a receipt to add a receipt’s foods for you to check, or Import a recipe to save a recipe to My foods and put a serving in this meal.

How do I track water?

On the Food tab, tap Add a glass on the water card. You can change the glass size, remove a glass you added by mistake, and set an optional daily water target under Daily targets. Water is shown in fluid ounces or milliliters, depending on your region. The small Water widget can add a glass from your Home Screen too.

How do I scan a food barcode?

On the Food tab, tap Add food, then Scan a barcode. The first time, iOS asks for permission to use the camera. Then point your iPhone at the barcode on the package and the app reads it by itself.

  • Find good light, and avoid glare on shiny wrappers.
  • Hold steady a few inches away, with the whole barcode in view. Pinch to zoom in on a small one.
  • Flatten curved or crumpled packaging as best you can.

If it still won’t read, tap Type it instead and type the numbers printed under the stripes (8 to 14 digits), then tap Look it up. You can also choose Type a barcode straight from the Add food screen.

If camera access is off, turn it on in the iOS Settings app under Apps → Losing It → Camera, or just type the numbers.

Looking up a barcode needs an internet connection. The app checks our food index first, which includes nutrition labels shared by food makers, and asks it by a bucket number rather than the barcode itself. If the product isn’t there, or the index can’t be reached, the app sends just the barcode number to Open Food Facts.

A product wasn’t found, or it has no calories.

Product details come from our food index and from Open Food Facts, an open food database filled in by volunteers, so some products are missing or incomplete.

  • Not found: the app opens a short form so you can add it by hand with a name and the calories from the label. It keeps the barcode, so the next time you scan that product the app fills it in from your own log.
  • No calories listed: the app shows what it found and asks you to type in the number from the label.
  • Calories don’t match your package: tap Adjust the details before you add it, or tap the food later on the Food tab to edit it.
  • Lookup didn’t go through: check your connection and tap Try again, or tap Add it yourself.

You can always skip the barcode entirely: tap Add food, then Add it yourself.

How do I set, change or turn off my daily targets?

Daily targets are completely optional, and they’re all off until you set one. Without them, the Food tab is a simple log.

  • Set or change them: on the Food tab, tap Set daily targets (or Change targets). You can also open Settings (the gear icon on the Journey tab) and tap Daily targets under Food. Fill in only the ones you want: calories, protein, carbs, fat, fiber and water. Then tap Save my targets.
  • Split my calories: if you like thinking in percentages, this optional helper turns your own percentages into grams for protein, carbs and fat.
  • Turn them off: clear any field, or tap Turn off all my targets. Your log stays just as it is.

The calorie target can be anything from 1,200 to 5,000 calories a day. Losing It never suggests a number, so please talk with your doctor about what’s right for you. If a day goes over a target, the app simply says that’s okay. Tomorrow is a fresh page.

How do I reset and start over?

Open Settings (the gear icon on the Journey tab), scroll to the bottom and tap Erase everything & start fresh, then confirm. This deletes your journey, weigh-ins, photos, food log, water log, and saved foods, recipes and meals, and clears the widgets, reminders and any recipes waiting to be checked.

If iCloud sync is on, this also erases your journal from iCloud and from every device signed in to that iCloud account. It can’t be undone.

To remove Losing It from one iPhone only, delete the app from that iPhone. Your iCloud copy stays and comes back if you reinstall.

Can I use kilograms?

Not yet. Losing It currently tracks weight in pounds only.

My weigh-in reminders aren’t showing up.

Turn on Daily weigh-in reminder in the app’s Settings and pick a time. If notifications are off for Losing It, Settings in the app lets you know; turn them on in the iOS Settings app under Notifications → Losing It.

Is there anything else to buy?

No. Losing It is a one-time purchase with no subscriptions, no in-app purchases, no ads and no account.

Is Losing It medical advice?

No. Losing It is a personal journal for tracking and encouragement. It isn’t a medical device and doesn’t provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment, and Glow Coach notes and affirmations are encouragement, not health guidance.

The food log is the same. It doesn’t give diet or nutrition advice, the calorie target is a number you choose rather than a recommendation, and calorie numbers from labels, food databases, imported recipes or receipts may not be exact.

Talk with a doctor or other qualified health professional before starting or changing a weight-loss plan, especially if you are pregnant, have a health condition, or have a history of disordered eating.

Privacy

Losing It has no account, no ads and no tracking, and we never see your data. Food search never sends the words you type. A barcode lookup, whether you scan it, type it or it’s printed on a receipt, sends only the barcode number to Open Food Facts, and only when our food index doesn’t have the product or can’t be reached. Recipes and receipts you import are read on your iPhone, and the photos, scans, PDFs and text themselves are never uploaded. Anything you save from them, or add to your day, syncs through your own private iCloud like the rest of your journal, if iCloud is on. To read a recipe from a link, or a page from Safari that didn’t include its recipe, your iPhone opens that page straight from its website. If the recipe lives on another site that page points to, it opens that one page too. Either way, it sends no cookies and nothing from your journal. Read the full privacy policy.
